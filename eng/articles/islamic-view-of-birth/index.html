🌙 Islam & Birth

The Islamic View of Birth: Between Honor and Humility

How does Islam understand the moment of birth and the meaning of celebrating it?

8 min read

When a child is born in a Muslim community, religious rituals of deep meaning are set in motion. These aren't merely social customs — they are doctrinal expressions of Islam's position on the human being and the value of their existence.

The Adhan: The First Word

The first sound a Muslim newborn hears is the adhan called into their right ear — a declaration that they enter the world under God's name and the word of monotheism. Before knowing anything of this world, they know that God is the Greatest. This is the deepest identity declaration imaginable.

The Seventh Day: Aqiqah and Naming

The aqiqah is not merely a feast — it carries deep religious meaning: gratitude to God for the blessing of the newborn, and charity sharing that joy with the community. The name chosen is an identity declaration, not merely an aesthetic choice. "The most beloved names to God are Abdullah and Abd al-Rahman."

The Hijri Calendar and Spiritual Memory

Born in Ramadan? In the month of the Quran and forgiveness. In Dhul Hijjah? In the ten days most beloved to God. In Rabi' al-Awwal? In the month of the Prophet's birth ﷺ. The lunar calendar weaves personal birth into the spiritual rhythm of the entire Islamic year.

Your Hijri birthday is not a guide to your personality — but it is a window onto the rhythm of the civilization you belong to.
Sources: "Rulings on the Newborn" by Dr. Umar Al-Ashqar, Permanent Committee Fatwas, IslamQA.info
